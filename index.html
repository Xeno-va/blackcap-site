How it works

Three steps, and the third one is reading your phone.

1

Clip it on

One clamp around one motor wire, the unit inside the cabinet, the antenna outside. Your electrician does it in one visit, with the motor locked out.

2

It reads every stroke

Your motor works harder on the upstroke than the downstroke, and the shape of that effort says what is happening downhole. Blackcap reads it all day and sends it over the cellular network.

3

Read your morning list

Before you leave the house: which wells are fine, which need a look, and why. The route you drive is the route that matters.

Out of the hazard zone, out of the way.

Blackcap lives in the motor starter cabinet, well away from the wellhead. No wellhead fittings, no load cell on the polished rod, nothing on the pumping unit to shake loose or get hit.

It runs from the cabinet's own power and needs no Wi-Fi, no solar panel and no battery to swap.

Where small wells lose money

Four problems nobody sees between visits.

A well goes down and sits

A breaker trips on Friday and the well is found on Monday's route: three days of oil that never reach the tank. Blackcap sees the motor stop. That well is at the top of your list the same morning, with when it stopped.

The pump keeps running after the well is dry

Timers get set once and left. Every minute pumping an empty hole is power you pay for and a pump pounding the rods. Blackcap shows when each well pumps off and how long it ran past that, so the timer can be set to the well.

One power bill, no idea which well

Electricity is one of the biggest costs on a small well, and it arrives as one number. Blackcap measures each motor, so you see what every well costs to run, what that comes to per barrel, and which ones aren't paying for themselves.

A rod job with no warning

A parted rod or a worn-out pump means a pulling unit and days of lost oil. The motor feels it first. Blackcap compares every stroke to that well's own normal and flags the drift; a parted rod shows up the moment it happens.
